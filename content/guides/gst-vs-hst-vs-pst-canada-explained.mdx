# Canada Sales Tax Guide: GST vs HST vs PST Explained

Understanding Canadian sales taxes can be confusing because tax structures vary significantly from province to province. In Canada, sales taxes consist of three distinct types:

1. **GST (Goods and Services Tax)**: A 5% federal tax applied nationwide.
2. **PST (Provincial Sales Tax)**: A province-specific tax levied in British Columbia (7%), Manitoba (7%), Saskatchewan (6%), and Quebec (9.975% QST).
3. **HST (Harmonized Sales Tax)**: A blended single tax rate combining federal GST and provincial tax in Ontario (13%), Nova Scotia (15%), New Brunswick (15%), PEI (15%), and Newfoundland (15%).

## 2. How to Reverse Calculate Canadian Sales Tax

To calculate pre-tax net amounts from Canadian tax-inclusive receipts, use the following formulas:

### A. Harmonized Sales Tax (HST) Provinces (e.g. Ontario 13%)

`Pre-Tax Net Amount = Gross Receipt ÷ 1.13`
`HST Paid = Gross Receipt - Pre-Tax Net Amount`

**Worked Example:** An Ontario receipt of **$113.00 CAD** yields **$100.00 CAD** net price and **$13.00 CAD** HST.

### B. Dual-Tax Provinces (e.g. Quebec 14.975% GST + QST)

`Pre-Tax Net Amount = Gross Receipt ÷ 1.14975`
`GST (5%) = Pre-Tax Net Amount × 0.05`
`QST (9.975%) = Pre-Tax Net Amount × 0.09975`

**Worked Example:** A Quebec receipt of **$114.98 CAD** yields **$100.00 CAD** net price, **$5.00 CAD** GST, and **$9.98 CAD** QST.

## 3. Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) Input Tax Credits (ITCs)

Business registrants filing CRA GST/HST returns must keep accurate accounting records:
- **Eligible Expenses:** Commercial software, office supplies, utilities, and commercial rent.
- **50% Restriction Rule:** Meal and entertainment expense ITCs are limited to 50% under CRA rules.
- **Record Retention:** Retain all tax receipts with vendor Business Numbers (BN) for a minimum of 6 years.
