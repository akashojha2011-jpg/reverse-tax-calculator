# Net to Gross Salary Explained: Paycheck Math Guide

When discussing employment compensation, salary negotiations, or freelancing contracts, compensation is frequently expressed in terms of **net take-home pay** (the actual cash deposited into your bank account). However, corporate payroll systems operate on **gross salary** (the total pay before taxes and withholdings).

Understanding how to convert **net to gross** enables job seekers, employees, and contractors to negotiate salaries effectively and avoid underestimating tax obligations.

---

## 1. Net Pay vs. Gross Salary: What is the Difference?

- **Gross Salary**: The total agreed compensation offered by an employer before any payroll deductions or taxes are withheld.
- **Net Take-Home Pay**: The residual income received by the employee after federal income tax, state/provincial tax, social security (FICA/CPP/EI), and healthcare premiums are deducted.

---

## 2. The Net-to-Gross Calculation Formula

Unlike flat sales tax where tax is added to a net price (`Gross = Net × (1 + r)`), income taxes are deducted from gross pay (`Net = Gross × (1 - t)`).

To calculate the required **Gross Salary** from a desired **Net Pay**:

`Required Gross Salary = Target Net Pay ÷ (1 - Effective Tax Rate Decimal)`

`Tax Withheld = Required Gross Salary - Target Net Pay`

## 4. Key Factors Influencing Payroll Tax Withholding

1. **Progressive Marginal Tax Brackets**: Tax rates increase as income rises across tax brackets.
2. **Social Security & Medicare (FICA)**: In the US, 6.2% Social Security + 1.45% Medicare (7.65% total) is withheld from gross wages.
3. **State & Local Income Taxes**: State tax rates range from 0% (Texas, Florida, Washington, Nevada) to 13.3% in California.
4. **Pre-Tax Deductions**: 401(k) retirement contributions, HSA/FSA contributions, and health insurance premiums reduce taxable gross income.
